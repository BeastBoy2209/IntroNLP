1. Executive Summary & Research Motivation¶

1.1 The Operational Challenge¶

The proliferation of decentralised digital communication channels (social media platforms, group chats, discussion forums) has exacerbated the volume of non-normative online content, specifically abusive/toxic commentary and unsolicited commercial/malicious spam. Manual content moderation presents three critical bottlenecks:

  1. Throughput & Latency: Human review cannot scale linearly with millions of user-generated events per hour.
  2. Psychological & Occupational Hazard: Persistent exposure to toxic and violent text results in severe cognitive fatigue and distress for human moderators.
  3. Inconsistency: Subjective variations between moderators lead to non-deterministic policy enforcement.

1.2 System Scope & Classification Taxonomy¶

Our objective is to engineer an end-to-end, real-time Natural Language Processing pipeline that classifies incoming textual events into three distinct operational states:

  • Normal: Benign, contextually constructive conversational text.
  • Toxic: Abusive, obscene, aggressive, threatening, or hateful utterances requiring content filtering or warnings.
  • Spam: Unsolicited advertisements, phishing attempts, bot broadcasts, and repetitive commercial noise.

1.3 Milestone 5 Objectives¶

  1. Data Ingestion & Integrity Auditing: Ingest the Wikipedia-derived Jigsaw Toxic Comment dataset and SMS Spam corpus.
  2. Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA): Quantify class imbalance, text length dynamics, syntactic patterns (capitalisation, punctuation), and lexical markers.
  3. Multi-label Topology Analysis: Investigate how distinct sub-categories of toxicity (threat, obscenity, insult, hate) co-occur within the raw Jigsaw corpus.
  4. Stratified Partitioning: Execute a leakage-free 80% Training / 20% Test partition preserving prior distributions.

3.2 Evaluation Metric Implications¶

Critical Finding: The dataset exhibits a 70.3% : 28.5% : 1.2% imbalance ratio.

  • A naive majority-class baseline would obtain an Accuracy of ~70.3% while completely failing to detect any harmful or spam messages.
  • Therefore, Accuracy is an invalid primary performance indicator.
  • For Weeks 7–9, model evaluation will formally prioritize Macro-averaged F1-Score and Class-Weighted Cross-Entropy / Loss Weights to ensure equal penalty across minority classes.

To understand what vocabulary drives discriminability before building TF-IDF representations in Week 7, we compute the top non-stopword tokens for each class.

# Basic stopword list to extract content-bearing tokens
basic_stopwords = set([
    'the', 'a', 'an', 'and', 'or', 'in', 'on', 'at', 'to', 'for', 'of', 'with', 
    'is', 'are', 'was', 'were', 'it', 'this', 'that', 'i', 'you', 'he', 'she', 
    'we', 'they', 'my', 'your', 'have', 'has', 'be', 'as', 'do', 'not', 'but', 
    'from', 'by', 'will', 'can', 'if', 'me', 'so', 'what', 'about', 'just'
])

def extract_top_ngrams(df, label, n=15):
    subset = df[df['label'] == label]['text'].dropna()
    tokens = []
    for text in subset:
        words = re.findall(r'\b[a-zA-Z]{3,}\b', text.lower())
        tokens.extend([w for w in words if w not in basic_stopwords])
    return Counter(tokens).most_common(n)

top_normal = extract_top_ngrams(train_df, 'Normal')
top_toxic  = extract_top_ngrams(train_df, 'Toxic')
top_spam   = extract_top_ngrams(train_df, 'Spam')

6.1 Lexical Discriminators:¶

  • Normal: Dominated by collaborative and encyclopaedic terminology (article, page, edit, talk, wikipedia, please, think).
  • Toxic: Characterised by profanities, derogatory adjectives, and direct personal second-person attacks (fuck, shit, hate, moron, stupid).
  • Spam: Dominated by commercial urgency, prizes, and contact requests (call, free, txt, claim, won, mobile, prize, reply).

7. Conclusions & Strategic Roadmap for Week 6¶

7.1 Key Findings from Week 5 Milestone¶

  1. Corpus Balance: Class imbalance is pronounced (Normal: 70.3%, Toxic: 28.5%, Spam: 1.2%). This mandates stratified sampling and Macro-F1 scoring.
  2. Distinct Modalities:
    • Spam exhibits distinctive character caps (SMS standard) and specific token triggers.
    • Toxic comments contain higher emotional arousal signals (capital letters, exclamation marks, profanity clusters).
  3. Partition Verification: An 80/20 stratified split is established in data/processed/ with identical class ratios between train and test.

7.2 Week 6 Implementation Directives¶

  1. Text Preprocessing Pipeline:
    • URL & HTML entity stripping (critical for spam filtering).
    • Contraction expansion (don't $\rightarrow$ do not).
    • Normalisation and tokenisation.
  2. Selective Filtering:
    • Preserve profanity roots and sentiment modifiers (e.g. keeping negation words during stopword removal).
  3. Feature Engineering Readiness (Week 7):
    • Word-level and character-level n-gram TF-IDF vectorisation to mitigate out-of-vocabulary misspellings often found in toxic discourse.
